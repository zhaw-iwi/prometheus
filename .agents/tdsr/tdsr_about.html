Storyline

Gigi learns what it means to be useful around humans.

Gigi, a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, travels across Switzerland by car to discover how robots can live, learn, and collaborate with humans. At first, Gigi can walk, talk, and gesture, but lacks social intelligence, cultural awareness, and a clear purpose.

Through encounters with Swiss research institutions, engineers, researchers, and local people, Gigi gradually learns how to touch the world, move among people, work with humans, understand social cues, and find a meaningful role in society.

Gigi's Character Arc

From capable machine to context-aware collaborator

Initial State

Gigi starts as a capable but incomplete system: curious, slightly naive, technically impressive, but socially uncertain.

Development

At each research and cultural stop, Gigi gains a new layer of competence: physical intelligence, safe navigation, collaboration, social understanding, and cultural awareness.

Final State

Gigi does not become "human." Instead, Gigi becomes a more useful, trusted, and context-aware robotic collaborator.

Central question What is my role among humans?
Tool Observer Collaborator Companion

Storyline Overview

The journey moves through curiosity, learning, and integration.

Beginning: Curiosity

Gigi enters the journey "as-is": neutral, non-Swiss, observant, and occasionally awkward. The car becomes the recurring transition space where Gigi processes what has just happened and prepares for the next encounter.

Middle: Learning

The route alternates between research institutions and touristic or cultural stops. Research stops give Gigi explicit capabilities. Cultural stops test those capabilities in the real world, with real people and unscripted situations.

End: Integration

The final scene mirrors the opening, but Gigi has changed. Gigi moves more naturally, interacts more appropriately, and demonstrates a clearer sense of purpose: helping humans through collaboration, not replacing them.

Route Principle

Technical learning should be grounded in human settings.

  1. Research stopsGigi learns a specific capability.
  2. Touristic or cultural stopsGigi applies that learning in a human setting.

This rhythm keeps the film from becoming a sequence of lab visits. Each technical contribution is immediately grounded in Swiss landscapes, culture, public life, and everyday human interaction.

Final Scene

The ending mirrors the opening with a different quality of presence.

The final scene should mirror the opening car scene. In the beginning, Gigi enters the journey as a machine looking for orientation. At the end, Gigi returns to the car, a public place, or a shared task with a different quality of presence: calmer, more attentive, more useful, and more aware of humans.

The future of robotics is not robots replacing humans, but robots learning how to support, collaborate with, and adapt to people.
