# Alexandre de Spindler

## Biography

Prof. Alexandre de Spindler is an applied researcher with over two decades of experience in designing and evaluating interactive systems at the intersection of information systems, human-computer interaction (HCI), and human-AI collaboration. His work develops a coherent trajectory from early research on distributed and cooperative systems to current advances in structured, multi-party, and embodied human-AI interaction.

His early work established foundations in stateful and model-driven system design, including object databases and mobile information systems, and introduced collection-oriented programming as a paradigm for structuring dynamic data interactions (de Spindler, 2010a, 2010b). This research also explored cooperative and cross-media information systems, awareness support, and ambient interaction (de Spindler et al., 2009a, 2009b; Leone et al., 2010; Grossniklaus et al., 2010), forming the conceptual basis for later work on coordination and interaction in complex environments.

Since 2010, he has led and co-led numerous applied research projects funded by Innosuisse, DIZH, EU AAL, and institutional innovation programs. These projects consistently combine participatory design, prototyping, and real-world validation across healthcare, digital services, training, and collaborative work.

In healthcare, projects such as *Digital Companion* (Innosuisse, 2022a), *Tele-Assessment* (DIZH, 2022), and *Sleep Coach* (Innosuisse, 2021), as well as the EU AAL project *FairCare* (EU AAL, 2015), develop and validate AI-supported patient interaction and assessment systems. This work is reflected in research on digital health interventions and clinical feasibility studies (Unger et al., 2025; Färber et al., 2023; Meng et al., 2024). It also includes virtual-reality and dialogue-oriented approaches to patient education for chronic diseases (Brucker-Kley et al., 2026).

In parallel, he has advanced conversational interaction systems through projects such as *Conversational Interactions for Business Process Automation* (Innosuisse, 2024a), *Conversational Interactions Supporting Content Production* (Innosuisse, 2024b), and earlier work on dialogue-based financial services (Innosuisse, 2016), demonstrating how language-based interfaces restructure workflows and user interaction.

His work on training and collaboration includes *Virtual Reality for Conflict Management Training* (Innosuisse, 2024c) and a virtual escape-room framework for team diagnostics (Innosuisse, 2020), investigating how interactive and immersive systems support team processes and decision-making.

Recent projects extend these approaches to AI-enabled domain applications, including *Detection of Applicable Laws using Language Models* (Innosuisse, 2023a), *Multisided EdTech Platform* (Innosuisse, 2023b), and *AI-enabled Sustainable Finance Advisory* (Innosuisse, 2025a), demonstrating the applicability of language technologies across domains.

Building on this applied foundation, his recent research introduces structured approaches to human-AI interaction. The PROMISE framework enables model-driven, state-machine-based orchestration of large language models to ensure predictable, testable conversational behaviour (Wu et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2023). PROMISE has been validated across healthcare, business process automation, and content production systems (Heierli et al., 2025; Innosuisse, 2024a; Innosuisse, 2024b).

This work also addresses the persuasive capabilities of digital agents. *Persuasive Prompting: The Case of Digital Health* shows how prompt design influences user engagement and behaviour in health-related interactions, providing empirical grounding for systems such as *Digital Companion* (Wu et al., 2025).

PROMISE has been extended to multi-party settings, enabling agents to monitor and support group processes through capabilities such as topic clustering, convergence detection, and structured reporting. This is reflected in projects such as *Digital Co-Facilitation to Increase the Efficiency of Team Work through Shared Insight and Improved Method Adherence* (Innosuisse, 2025b) and related research on coordination support (Grünert et al., 2026).

Complementing structured interaction control, the SBR framework enables socially adaptive agent behaviour by regulating tone, timing, and interaction strategies in human-AI interaction (de Spindler, 2024d). This line of work also includes simulation-based approaches to social behaviour coaching (Komenda et al., 2025) and conflict management training (Innosuisse, 2024c).

Current work focuses on PROMETHEUS, a next-generation framework that integrates structured interaction control and social behaviour regulation with multimodal sensing and behaviour generation, enabling real-time, multi-user interaction in collaborative and embodied settings (de Spindler, 2026a).

These frameworks are implemented as open-source software and validated across applied projects. They have also informed numerous student theses and live demonstrations, providing a foundation for reliable and socially responsive AI systems. Across these contributions, Prof. de Spindler’s research establishes a unified approach to designing controllable, socially adaptive, and multimodal AI agents that support human collaboration in complex, real-world environments.
